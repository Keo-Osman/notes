# Nuclear Model
The nucleus of an atom contains positive protons with charge $+e$ and uncharged neutrons. For a particular atom the *nucleus* is represented as 
$$
\ce{^{A}_{Z}X}
$$
Where $X$ is the chemical symbol, $A$ is the nucleon (total number of neutrons and protons) and $Z$ is the **atomic number** *(number of protons)*. Then the number of neutrons $N = A - Z$.

## Nuclear Size and Density
The radius of a nucleus is given by
$$
R = r_{0}A^{1/3}
$$
$r_{0}$ is the case where $A = 1$ e.g. radius of $\ce{^{1}_{1}H}$ nucleus - the radius of a proton.

# Strong Nuclear Force
The strong nuclear force is the force that holds the nucleus together as the protons repel from their like charge and the gravitational force is far too weak.
![[strong-nuclear-force-graph.png]]
The force is attractive between $0.5$ - $3\text{fm}$ and repulsive below $0.5\text{fm}$. with maximum attractive strength around $1\text{fm}$.

# Standard Model
The standard model is the set of *fundamental particles* - particles that can't be divided into anything smaller.


![[standard-model.png]]
**Leptons** are particles and anti-particles that are not affected by the strong nuclear force.
**Hadrons** are particles that are affected by the strong nuclear force.

## Antimatter
Every particle has it's antiparticle counter part. It has the same rest mass but opposite charge. Antimatter is often denoted with a bar e.g. $\bar{\nu}$.
## Fundamental Forces
There are 4 fundamental forces: gravity, strong nuclear, weak nuclear, electromagnetic.

# Beta Decay

## $\beta^{-}$ Decay
In $\beta^{-}$ decay a neutron decays into a proton, electron and electron anti-neutrino 
$$\ce{^{1}_{0}n}\to \ce{^{1}_{1}p} + \ce{^{0}_{-1}e} + \bar{\nu}_{e}$$
The nucleon number $A$ and atomic number $Z$ are conserved.
Zooming in more detail, thinking of the quark transformation we get
$$
\text{d}\to \text{u} + \ce{^{0}_{-1}e} + \bar{\nu}_{e}
$$
## $\beta^{+}$ Decay
In $\beta^{+}$ decay a neutron decays into a proton, positron and electron neutrino 
$$\ce{^{1}_{1}p}\to \ce{^{1}_{0}n} + \ce{^{0}_{+1}e} + \nu_{e}$$
$$\text{u}\to \text{d} + \ce{^{0}_{+1}e} + \nu_{e}$$
